Privacy notice

This notice describes data processing in the Android game QuaTD: Sci-Fi Tower Defense in version 1.0.19 (26) and on this information website. Section 16 describes what differed in the previous version 1.0.18 (25). The game is offered worldwide and is not directed at children under 13 (section 14).

The game itself does not require a separate QuaTD account. Game saves are stored locally. Optional Google services, the Google libraries built into the app, the optional “Ad-free” purchase through Google Play and externally opened support services may process data outside your device.

2. Local saves and backups

The app uses shared_preferences to store data in local app storage. This includes:

  • highscores, experience points and diamonds;
  • mission stars, unlocks including unlocked mission solutions, talents, research and achievements;
  • the state of an interrupted Endless run, so that you can continue it;
  • tutorial progress and language, audio and display settings;
  • the age group you chose for advertising, or your choice to continue without ads (section 3);
  • the status of the “Ad-free” option (unlocked, source of the unlock and time) and notes on whether a payment or the purchase acknowledgement to Google is still pending and since when Google Play has no longer reported a purchase (section 4);
  • if you are connected to Play Games: your Play Games player ID, your most recently retrieved world rank, the number of players and up to eleven neighbouring leaderboard entries with rank, score and the public display name of other players (section 5);
  • dates and times, daily advertising limits and reward receipts that prevent duplicate credits.

In addition, the Google UMP SDK stores your advertising privacy choices in local app storage (section 3).

These data support saved progress, settings and correct reward allocation. The audited app has no operator-run server receiving the complete game save. Transmission of Endless scores to Google Play Games is described in section 5.

Android/Google backups: Depending on your device, operating system and backup settings, Android or Google may back up app data or transfer it to another device. The app does not expressly disable these backups, and they may also include your age group, the Ad-free status and the leaderboard cache. We therefore cannot assure you that local data will always remain exclusively on the original device. You can manage backup settings through your device or Google account.

3. Advertising and privacy choices

QuaTD uses Google AdMob / Google Mobile Ads for optional rewarded ads. They are offered for diamonds (the “Rewards” screen), a bonus in the “Daily Case”, doubling the reward after a completed Endless run (result screen), a talent respec and unlocking a mission solution. Watching an ad is not required for the basic game. The audited integration contains no banner, interstitial or app-open ads.

The version distributed through Google Play uses live rewarded ads from Google; this also applies to versions on internal or closed testing tracks. Only builds explicitly created with test configuration use Google test ads; test ads may also involve data processing by the advertising SDK. While the “Ad-free” option (section 4) is active, the app requests no ads.

According to Google, the Mobile Ads SDK may in particular process:

  • IP addresses, from which an approximate location may be inferred;
  • interactions with the app and advertising, such as views, clicks and video views;
  • technical diagnostic and performance data;
  • advertising IDs, app set IDs and, where applicable, other device- or account-related identifiers.

Google identifies advertising, analytics and fraud prevention as purposes. Processing in an individual case depends in part on SDK, account and privacy settings. In the audited ad request, the app does not supply a separate QuaTD account ID. It transmits the age-related protection signals described below to Google.

Age group: On first launch, before the main menu, the app asks you to optionally choose your age group: under 16, 16–17, or 18 and over. Alternatively, you can choose “Continue without ads”. Nothing is preselected, and the app does not ask for your date of birth. While the “Ad-free” option is active, this question does not appear. If you close it without choosing, it appears again on the next launch; until then, the app requests no ads. The app stores the group, or your choice, in local app storage. It derives protection signals for Google: for under 16, the stricter child treatment and the UMP under-age-of-consent flag, with which, according to Google, no consent is requested; for 16–17, teen treatment; for 18 and over, no additional age restriction. These signals are transmitted with the corresponding Google requests and may convey information about your age group. For all groups, the app limits requested ad content to Google’s G rating. The age selection has been included since version 1.0.16 (23).

Without an age selection, the app requests no ads; the basic game remains playable, but optional video bonuses are unavailable. The purchased “Ad-free” option retains its direct rewards. In Settings → Ad privacy, you can see and change your age group or turn off further ad requests; Google’s privacy options are also there (see below). Turning ads off does not retroactively delete data already transmitted to Google and does not automatically withdraw consent given to Google; the privacy options serve that purpose. This choice concerns advertising; Play Games, purchases and externally opened services have separate flows.

Consent status, form and preloading: If you can see ads (Android, an age group chosen, ad requests not turned off, “Ad-free” not active), the app requests your current consent status through the Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) in the background shortly after the first frame of every app start, as Google recommends. No form appears at that point, and the Mobile Ads SDK is not started. Google’s consent form opens only after you tap a video offer, never by itself when a screen opens; time spent reading the form does not count against loading time limits. Whether a form appears and which form is shown depends in particular on your region, previously saved choices and the Google configuration. Only when UMP permits ad requests does the app start the Mobile Ads SDK and request an ad. Once that is the case, the app preloads ads as soon as you open the “Rewards” screen or the “Daily Case”, or when the result screen after an Endless run appears; for the talent respec and mission solutions, it loads an ad only after you tap.

Even if you do not consent in the form, the app may subsequently request ads. Which serving mode Google then uses, such as non-personalised or limited ads, depends on your choices and the permission returned by UMP; these serving modes also process personal data such as your IP address. The audited ad request does not enforce a general “non-personalised ads only” setting. Google test ads use a separate flow without this UMP form. The form is provided by Google and itself loads fonts from Google Fonts (section 8).

Privacy options: In Settings → Ad privacy, the app shows a “Google privacy options” area, including while “Ad-free” is active and when ad requests are turned off. Where Google provides privacy options for your region, you can use “Privacy options” there to reopen, change or withdraw your choices at any time. When this screen opens, the app requests the consent status from Google once per session, also in these cases; if Google cannot be reached, you can try again there. After a change, the app no longer shows any previously preloaded ad. If you can see ads, the app also shows this access on the “Rewards” and “Daily Case” screens where Google provides it. You can also manage the advertising ID through the applicable Android settings.

Further information: Google Privacy Policy, Mobile Ads SDK data processing and Google UMP.

4. “Ad-free” purchase through Google Play

You can optionally buy the “Ad-free” option as a one-time in-app purchase. Purchase and payment are handled entirely by Google Play Billing. Payment details such as card or account information are not entered in QuaTD, and the app does not receive them. Google processes the data needed for the purchase under the Google Privacy Policy and the Google Play terms.

The app receives a purchase confirmation from Google Play, including a purchase identifier, and acknowledges the purchase to Google. Locally it stores that the option is unlocked, where the unlock came from and when it was saved, plus notes on a pending payment, a pending acknowledgement and checks for a possible loss of entitlement; it does not store the purchase identifier permanently. The app checks purchase status with Google Play, including at startup, when returning to the app and when restoring a purchase. This allows it to restore a valid purchase after reinstallation or on another device.

Pending payments, acknowledgements and refunds: While Google Play reports a payment as pending, the app does not unlock the option and does not acknowledge the purchase; once the payment completes, the unlock follows. When checking ownership, the app evaluates Google’s actual response code; connection or service errors never count as “no purchase”. The unlock is stored before the app acknowledges the purchase to Google; if the acknowledgement fails, the app retries it during the current session and at later starts and returns to the app. If a purchase was refunded or revoked, the app removes “Ad-free” only after Google Play has confirmed the absence of the purchase at least twice, at least one hour apart; a manual restore never removes an unlock. QuaTD does not run its own server for purchases.

While the option is active, the app requests no ads and does not call up an advertising consent form. Rewards are credited directly; the daily limits stay the same. A video preloaded before the purchase is discarded and no longer shown; the reward is credited directly. You can still change earlier consent choices in Settings → Ad privacy (section 3). The Google Mobile Ads SDK remains part of the app (section 8). All versions distributed through Google Play, including internal and closed tests, use the real Google Play purchase.

More information: Google Privacy Policy and Google Play — purchases and refunds.

5. Google Play Games

On Android, Google Play Games Services v2 is integrated for sign-in and the Endless leaderboard. The SDK is initialised when the app starts. If you already have a Play Games profile on the device, Google Play Games signs you in automatically at that point, without any action in QuaTD; this is Google’s standard behaviour. If you have no profile, the app does not create one automatically; you can then sign in through the leaderboard feature. Google sign-in is not required for normal gameplay.

The app uses the Play Games player ID for association and to detect an account change, and stores it locally. After a valid completed Endless run, its final score is submitted to Google automatically, without a separate prompt, if connected. Completing missions or merely loading a saved local highscore does not trigger this submission.

Automatic leaderboard retrieval: If connected, the app automatically retrieves your world rank, the number of players and up to eleven neighbouring entries of the public leaderboard at startup and after every score submission. These entries contain the rank, score and public Play Games display name of other players. The app displays them and stores the latest retrieval locally so that it can also be shown offline. “Reset Progress” does not delete this cache; it is only removed when the app data are cleared or the app is uninstalled. The full leaderboard opens in Google’s own interface.

QuaTD has no separate password-based account system. The audited flow does not read Google passwords, email addresses, friend lists or Google cloud game saves; only the public leaderboard entries described above are read. Google additionally processes its own profile, analytics and diagnostic data for Play Games. Visibility of your profile and game information, including your own leaderboard entry for other players, depends on your Google profile settings.

Deleting local progress does not remove leaderboard data already stored by Google. You can separately manage or delete your Play Games data through your Play Games profile or Google account. Further information: Google Play Games — data processing and deletion.

6. Voluntary tip through PayPal

A voluntary tip to the developer is possible through the separate “Tip the developer” page, which you reach from the “Credits” screen (via Settings). It is separate from the “Ad-free” purchase; the page with the “Ad-free” purchase contains no PayPal link. The tip is explicitly voluntary and grants no content, features, badges or other advantages in the game. If you select “PayPal” there, the app opens PayPal through an external handler, such as your browser. There is no PayPal SDK and no payment processing within QuaTD. The app does not receive or store payment status or payment details through this flow.

When you visit PayPal and make a voluntary payment, PayPal processes data under its own terms. Outside the app, the recipient may receive information about the payment and the payer. The specific information depends on the PayPal transaction; this notice therefore does not claim that payments are anonymous. See the PayPal Privacy Statement.

8. Permissions and technical data

The Android app has internet/network permissions, SDK permissions for advertising identifiers and Android advertising services, including Android Privacy Sandbox interfaces (Topics, Attribution Reporting) that the Google SDK may use, plus the Google Play Billing permission for the “Ad-free” purchase. The audited release manifest contained no access permissions for precise device location, contacts, camera or microphone. Approximate location may nevertheless be inferred from an IP address.

Start component of the advertising SDK: The app manifest registers a start component of the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which Android loads on every app start, including while the “Ad-free” option is active, without an age selection and before any privacy choice. According to the operator’s review, it only reads the app’s configuration; in the measurement described below, there was no traffic to advertising or consent servers at startup. The app only starts the Mobile Ads SDK itself once UMP permits ad requests (section 3).

Diagnostic data of the Google Play Billing Library: The Google Play Billing Library (version 8.0.0), built in for the “Ad-free” purchase, sends technical diagnostic data to Google through Google’s DataTransport service (server firebaselogging.googleapis.com). In the measurement, this happened about 40 seconds after app start, without any action on your part and independently of a purchase. The operator does not receive the content of this transmission. It is a function of the Google library, not a Firebase product integrated by the operator.

The app writes technical status and error messages, for example about ads, Google authentication and score submission, to local diagnostic output. No operator-run remote log upload or analytics backend was identified. Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics are not integrated. This does not exclude diagnostic or analytics processing by the Google SDKs themselves (see above).

Fonts and game media are distributed with the app; automatic downloading of Google Fonts by the app is disabled. Google’s consent form (section 3), however, itself loads fonts from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com); Google receives your IP address, among other things, in the process. The version display reads the running app’s version and build number; it does not call a separate web service for this purpose.

Network measurement: On 14 October 2026, the operator recorded the network traffic of version 1.0.18 after a fresh installation on an Android emulator located in Germany. On first launch without any input, the app connected only to firebaselogging.googleapis.com (billing diagnostics, see above), not to AdMob or UMP servers. After an age group was chosen, there was no traffic for 45 seconds. When “Rewards” was opened, the app queried UMP (fundingchoicesmessages.google.com) and Google’s consent form appeared by itself; while it was open, there were no connections other than UMP and Google Fonts and no ad request. After “Do not consent” and another tap, the Mobile Ads SDK started and requested ads (including googleads.g.doubleclick.net). The measurement is a snapshot; the flow may differ depending on region, device, Google configuration and earlier choices. It concerned version 1.0.18. Version 1.0.19 additionally requests the consent status from UMP at every start if you can see ads, and shows the form only after you tap a video offer (section 3).

9. Data security

According to Google, the integrated Google SDKs transmit data encrypted (TLS). The operator does not run a server that receives game, advertising or purchase data from the app. The app stores local data in private app storage, which other apps cannot access under Android’s security model (app sandbox); the app does not additionally encrypt these data. The app does not receive payment details and does not store purchase identifiers (purchase tokens). The app opens external pages such as PayPal, email drafts and this notice only for fixed, predefined addresses in external applications.

You can report security concerns to acidsstudio@gmail.com.

10. Retention and deletion

Local progress remains stored for later play sessions. “Reset Progress” deletes the designated progress data. In particular, it does not delete language, audio and other settings, your advertising age group, the “Ad-free” status, unlocked mission solutions, the leaderboard cache (section 5), the privacy choices stored by Google UMP or external account connections. These data remain stored until the app is uninstalled or its app data are cleared. Android provides functions to clear app data or uninstall the app. Existing backups and data held by external services may remain separately. The “Ad-free” purchase stays linked to your Google account; a local reset does not remove it, and Google Play continues to record it.

Data independently processed by Google, PayPal or GitHub is subject to their respective retention and deletion procedures. The app cannot erase all such data through a local reset.

Support correspondence: Resolved support emails are deleted no later than six months after the request is closed. If a message is still needed for an open case or to meet a statutory retention obligation, only the content necessary for that purpose is retained until that reason no longer applies.

Payment records: Payment and accounting records available to the operator are retained separately from ordinary support correspondence where and for as long as statutory retention obligations apply. The retention period depends on the type of record and the applicable tax and commercial-law requirements. The data are then deleted unless another statutory retention requirement applies. For access and erasure requests, use the privacy contact in section 1.

11. Legal bases and your rights

The operator is based in Germany. Processing by the operator relies in particular on the following legal bases under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR):

  • Gameplay, saved progress and settings: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR where necessary to provide the game functions you request.
  • Ad-free purchase, purchase acknowledgement and restoration: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR to carry out the purchase and provide the purchased feature. For integrating the Google Play Billing Library, whose diagnostic transmission to Google takes place independently of a purchase (section 8), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest is a reliably working purchase option.
  • Play Games functions while connected: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR for associating your player profile and submitting the Endless score to the leaderboard.
  • Automatic Play Games processes: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for the automatic sign-in of existing profiles at startup, the automatic retrieval of your world rank and neighbouring leaderboard entries including public display names of other players, and the local cache. The legitimate interest is showing an up-to-date leaderboard without extra steps and also offline, and detecting account changes. Google also processes SDK and account data as an independent controller under its privacy notices; technical SDK data may be processed during initialisation before an explicit sign-in.
  • Consent-dependent advertising: Article 6(1)(a) GDPR for processing for which the privacy form requests your consent. You can change or withdraw your choices through the privacy options described in section 3. Processing by Google and advertising partners also depends on the purposes and legal bases identified in that form.
  • Advertising without consent: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR where ads are served without your consent, for example as non-personalised or limited ads. The legitimate interest is financing the free game through optional reward videos and enabling their delivery and fraud prevention. Selecting non-personalised advertising does not mean that no data are processed.
  • Age group and age-related protection signals: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest is treating ads in an age-appropriate way and complying with the special protection requirements for minors, in particular Article 8 GDPR.
  • Requesting and storing your consent decision, including the status request at app start: Article 6(1)(c) in conjunction with Article 7(1) GDPR (demonstrating consent); the decision is stored on your device by the Google UMP SDK.
  • Support: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR where your request concerns a purchase or provision of the service; Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for general enquiries. The legitimate interest is answering enquiries and resolving problems.
  • Voluntary tip and accounting: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR for handling a tip you initiate, insofar as the operator receives data; Article 6(1)(c) GDPR for legally required record retention.
  • Secure operation and provision of this website: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest is reliably providing information and preventing abuse and technical attacks.

Storing information on or accessing information from your device is also governed by section 25 of the German TDDDG. Technically necessary operations to provide explicitly requested functions fall within its exceptions; operations requiring consent need your consent. Google, PayPal and GitHub explain their independent processing and legal bases in the linked privacy notices.

Provision of data: You are under no statutory or contractual obligation to provide data. Without an age group, the optional video bonuses are unavailable; without Play Games sign-in, the online leaderboard is unavailable; and the “Ad-free” purchase requires purchase processing by Google Play. The basic game remains usable in all cases.

Automated decision-making and profiling: The operator does not make decisions based solely on automated processing within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR and does not create profiles. Whether Google creates profiles for personalised advertising depends on your choices in the consent form, your region and your Google settings; Google explains the details in the linked notices.

Where the GDPR applies and its statutory conditions are met, you may have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability and objection (section 12). Where processing is based on your consent, you may withdraw it for the future. This does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing.

Right to lodge a complaint: You may lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, in particular in the Member State of your habitual residence, place of work or place of the alleged infringement. The authority responsible for the operator is the State Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information of Baden-Württemberg (Landesbeauftragter für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit Baden-Württemberg).

For requests about processing by the operator, contact acidsstudio@gmail.com. Controls and contact channels provided by Google, PayPal and GitHub are also available for data they hold independently.

12. Right to object

Right to object under Article 21 GDPR: Where the operator processes your data on the basis of legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), you have the right to object to this processing at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. The operator will then no longer process the data unless it can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override your interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing serves the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.

Where data are processed for direct marketing purposes, you may object to this processing at any time without giving reasons.

You can send your objection informally to acidsstudio@gmail.com. You can also turn off ad requests yourself at any time in Settings → Ad privacy; Google’s privacy options are also there.

14. Children

QuaTD is not directed at children under 13. In Google Play, the operator has specified the age groups 13–15, 16–17 and 18 and over as the target audience. 16. Changes and language versions

This version describes version 1.0.19 (26). It is based on the source code of that version, a network measurement of the previous version 1.0.18 of 14 October 2026 (section 8) and information confirmed by the operator. The age selection has been included since version 1.0.16 (23). Changes to services or processing may require an update. The date at the top identifies this version.

Differences in version 1.0.18 (25): There, the app requested the consent status only when it first preloaded an ad, so Google’s consent form could appear by itself when “Rewards”, the “Daily Case” or the result screen opened. A video preloaded before the “Ad-free” purchase could still be played. If Google Play reported no purchase in a single query, the app removed the local unlock; failed acknowledgements were only retried at the next start or return to the app. The PayPal link was on the support page next to the “Ad-free” purchase.

The German, English, French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese versions describe the same practices. You can choose a language at the top of every page. From version 1.0.18 (25), the app opens this notice in the selected game language. Version 1.0.17 (24) and older versions open the English version for French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese; the translations are accessible from there.
